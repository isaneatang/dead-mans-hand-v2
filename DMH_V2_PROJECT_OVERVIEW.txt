================================================================================
DEAD MAN'S HAND v2 (DMH v2)
A Decentralized Crypto Inheritance & Emergency-Access Protocol on BOT Chain
================================================================================

PROJECT OVERVIEW & PROBLEM STATEMENT
Detailed explanation of what this project is, the problems it set out to
solve, the flaws discovered along the way, and how each one was resolved.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE HUMAN PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cryptocurrency has a death problem.

When the holder of a crypto wallet dies, goes missing, becomes incapacitated,
or simply loses access to their keys, their assets do not pass to their family
the way a bank account does. There is no customer support line, no probate
court order that a blockchain will obey, no "forgot password" link. The assets
sit at an address forever, mathematically visible to everyone and practically
reachable by no one. Industry estimates suggest millions of coins are already
permanently stranded this way.

The existing "solutions" all have fatal flaws:

  a) WRITE YOUR SEED PHRASE DOWN AND GIVE IT TO SOMEONE
     This gives that person full, immediate, irrevocable control of your
     funds while you are still alive. It is not inheritance; it is a
     handover. It also creates a single piece of paper whose theft equals
     total loss.

  b) CUSTODIAL SERVICES / EXCHANGES WITH BENEFICIARY FORMS
     You must trust a company to still exist, remain honest, remain solvent,
     and honor the arrangement decades from now. The entire point of
     self-custody is defeated.

  c) LAWYERS AND WILLS
     A will can name a beneficiary, but it cannot move coins. If the keys
     are lost, the will is a piece of paper describing money nobody can
     touch. If the keys are IN the will, everyone in the probate chain can
     read them.

  d) SMART-CONTRACT LOCKUPS
     Most on-chain inheritance tools require you to deposit your assets into
     a contract today. Your funds are now locked, illiquid, and exposed to
     that contract's bugs for years while you are still alive and using them.

DMH v2 exists to solve this: a way for assets to pass to a chosen person
after the owner goes silent, WITHOUT giving anyone power over the assets
while the owner is alive, WITHOUT trusting any company or server, WITHOUT
locking the assets up front, and WITHOUT the transfer mechanism itself
leaking the secret that guards it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT THE PROJECT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DMH v2 is a "dead man's switch" smart-contract protocol deployed on BOT Chain
(an EVM-compatible Layer 1; testnet chain ID 968, mainnet 677), plus a static
web frontend and an optional Python verification CLI.

The core mechanics:

  1. REGISTER, DON'T DEPOSIT (non-custodial by design)
     The owner creates a vault and registers tokens they already hold
     (ERC-20 and enumerable ERC-721). Nothing moves. The only thing granted
     is a standard token allowance (approve / setApprovalForAll) from the
     owner's own wallet to the vault contract. The owner keeps full custody
     and full use of every asset the entire time. The contract holds
     permission, never possession.

  2. PROVE YOU'RE ALIVE (the heartbeat)
     The owner picks an inactivity period (minutes for demos, up to 10 years
     for real use) and periodically calls ping() to reset the clock. As long
     as pings keep arriving, the vault is inert. The contract is completely
     passive — it does nothing on its own; it only answers when called.

  3. GO SILENT, BECOME CLAIMABLE
     If the inactivity period elapses with no ping, the vault becomes
     claimable. Nothing happens automatically — a claimant must show up.

  4. CLAIM WITH TWO SECRET PHRASES — WITHOUT EVER REVEALING THEM
     At setup, the owner composed two independent, original, high-entropy
     secret phrases (a self-written poem; a private kanji-and-translation
     pairing; any Unicode text in any language). Each phrase is run through
     a deliberately slow key-derivation function (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256,
     600,000 iterations) LOCALLY IN THE BROWSER to deterministically derive
     a private key. Only the two derived PUBLIC ADDRESSES were registered
     on-chain. The phrases and keys never left the owner's device.

     To claim, the beneficiary types the same two phrases. The frontend
     re-derives the same two keys (deterministic: same phrase in, same key
     out, forever, on any device). Each key signs a structured EIP-712
     message that binds together: the vault ID, the destination address the
     assets should go to, a one-time nonce, the chain ID, and the contract
     address. Only the two SIGNATURES are broadcast. The contract verifies
     both signatures against the two registered addresses via ecrecover,
     confirms the inactivity gate has passed, and sweeps the registered
     assets from the owner's wallet directly to the signed destination.

  5. FRICTION AGAINST ABUSE
     Every claim attempt costs a fee (in native BOT, forwarded instantly to
     a fee recipient — the contract never holds value). Failed attempts
     double the fee (capped) and put THAT CALLER on a self-expiring
     cooldown. Failure responses are deliberately uniform — the contract
     never reveals which of the two signatures was wrong.

The result: the chain acts purely as a WITNESS. It witnesses that a
commitment exists (two public addresses, a timer), and it verifies proofs
(signatures) — but it never has custody of the secrets or of the assets,
and nothing secret is ever transmitted through it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE TECHNICAL PROBLEMS IT SOLVES (AND HOW EACH WAS DISCOVERED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This project's design is the product of iteratively attacking its own first
version (DMH v1) until every discovered hole was closed. The problems below
are listed in the order they were found.

PROBLEM 1 — THE PLAINTEXT LEAK AT CLAIM TIME
  v1 stored a salted hash of a secret phrase on-chain (safe), but its claim
  function was attemptUnlock(vaultId, secretPlaintext) — the claimant had to
  submit the RAW PHRASE so the contract could hash and compare it. Every
  blockchain transaction, including its input data, is permanently public.
  The moment anyone claimed — including the legitimate beneficiary — the
  secret was burned into public transaction history forever. Even failed
  guesses were published. The very act of honest, intended use destroyed the
  secret at exactly the moment it mattered most.

  Why "just hash it on the frontend first" doesn't fix it: hashing is
  deterministic, so the submitted hash becomes a static, copyable password.
  Anyone who sees it in a transaction can replay it. Moving the hashing
  around just relocates the leak; it does not close it.

  Why "lock the vault instantly on first match" doesn't fix it: the leak
  happens at BROADCAST, not at execution. A transaction sits in the public
  mempool, visible to every node and every bot, BEFORE any contract code
  runs. No amount of fast contract logic can outrun information that already
  left the sender's control at the network layer.

  SOLUTION: never transmit the secret in any form. Prove knowledge of it
  instead. Two candidate technologies were evaluated:

    - ZK-PROOFS (prove "I know a value hashing to X" without revealing it):
      cryptographically sound and keeps the memorable-phrase UX, but
      requires custom circuits (Circom/Groth16), a circuit-unfriendly hash
      swap (keccak256 -> Poseidon), and carries the well-documented
      "under-constrained circuit" bug class — subtle circuit errors that
      pass all tests yet accept forged proofs. Too heavy and too risky a
      surface for this scope.

    - PHRASE-DERIVED ECDSA SIGNATURES (chosen): derive a private key from
      the phrase via a slow KDF; sign a claim message; the contract checks
      the signature with ecrecover — the single most battle-tested primitive
      in the EVM (it verifies every Ethereum transaction ever sent). Keeps
      the memorable-phrase UX, uses decades-old proven math, and reduces the
      custom-code risk to ordinary, testable software correctness.

PROBLEM 2 — MEMPOOL FRONT-RUNNING
  Blockchains order transactions by gas fee, not arrival time. Bots
  ("generalized front-runners") scan the public mempool 24/7 for pending
  transactions containing valid unlock data, copy the data, and resubmit it
  with a higher fee so the validator executes THEIR copy first. Gas cost is
  no deterrent: paying $2,000 in gas to steal a $50,000 vault is a trade any
  attacker takes. There is no protocol-level fee ceiling and BOT Chain uses
  a standard fee-priority mempool with no fair-ordering guarantee.

  SOLUTION: make copied data worthless instead of trying to win the race.
  The destination address is baked INSIDE the signed EIP-712 payload. If a
  bot copies the two signatures and front-runs the transaction, the contract
  still pays out ONLY to the destination that was signed — the attacker just
  paid the gas for the beneficiary's claim. Front-running becomes not merely
  difficult but economically pointless. (Side benefit: because authorization
  comes entirely from the signatures and not from msg.sender, anyone can
  submit the transaction — the derived addresses never need gas, and the
  claim is relayer-friendly.)

PROBLEM 3 — SIGNATURE REPLAY
  A signature is static data. Without protection, a signature seen once
  (e.g., in a partially-failed claim) could be resubmitted later.

  SOLUTION: a per-vault monotonic nonce lives inside the signed payload and
  is incremented by the contract on every executed claim, instantly
  invalidating all previously issued signatures.

PROBLEM 4 — CROSS-CHAIN / CROSS-CONTRACT REPLAY (found during prompt review)
  The conversational design signed keccak256(vaultId, destination, nonce) —
  with no chain ID and no contract address. Since this project launches on
  testnet in preparation for mainnet, a user reusing the same phrases on
  both networks could have a testnet signature replayed against the mainnet
  vault. SOLUTION: full EIP-712 domain separation (name, version, chainId,
  verifyingContract) in every digest — AND the chain ID is additionally
  mixed into the KDF salt, so testnet and mainnet derive entirely different
  keys from the same phrases. Two independent walls.

PROBLEM 5 — OFFLINE BRUTE-FORCE OF THE SECRET
  Everything on a public chain is visible: the vault, the owner, the two
  registered addresses. Because key derivation is deterministic, an attacker
  can guess phrases OFFLINE — derive a key per guess, compare the address —
  millions of times per second, silently, without ever touching the contract
  or triggering any fee or cooldown. On-chain friction cannot see this
  attack at all. Low-entropy secrets (pet names, birthdays, famous quotes,
  song lyrics, known poems) fall in seconds to hours because attackers build
  dictionaries from exactly that kind of public, meaningful text — and in
  an inheritance context the attacker may be someone who KNEW the owner,
  making "personal" facts the FIRST guesses, not the last.

  SOLUTION (layered, and honestly labeled as the one user-dependent layer):
    - TWO independent, SELF-COMPOSED, original phrases required — never
      optional single-phrase mode (users reliably choose convenience over
      security when offered a weak path). Needing both correct multiplies
      difficulty rather than adding it.
    - A deliberately SLOW KDF (600k-iteration PBKDF2) instead of a raw fast
      hash, cutting attacker guess rates by orders of magnitude.
    - Full Unicode support so phrases can be poems, kanji, any language —
      dramatically expanding the search space (and a genuine product
      differentiator).
    - Frontend entropy scoring, explicit warnings against personal facts
      and known text, and guidance to store the two phrases in SEPARATE
      places or with separate people — turning "two secrets" into true
      risk diversification, not one long password split in half.
  The honest limit, stated openly: no contract mechanism can save a weak
  phrase from offline guessing. Entropy is the user's contribution; the
  protocol maximizes the value of that entropy.

PROBLEM 6 — SIDE-CHANNEL LEAK ("WHICH PHRASE WAS WRONG?")
  If the contract or its events revealed that signature A verified but B
  failed, an attacker could brute-force each phrase INDEPENDENTLY — roughly
  square-rooting the total work and gutting the dual-phrase design.
  SOLUTION: uniform failure. Both signatures are always evaluated; any
  failure produces the identical event, identical fee escalation, identical
  cooldown, and no distinguishing error. (The claim UI does perform a LOCAL
  derived-address comparison to help a struggling legitimate claimant —
  this is safe because the comparison uses only public data an offline
  attacker already has; nothing extra is leaked on-chain.)

PROBLEM 7 — GRIEFING / DENIAL OF SERVICE ON THE REAL BENEFICIARY
  With signatures, on-chain guessing is useless to attackers — so v1's
  vault-wide cooldown flipped from a defense into a WEAPON: an attacker
  could spam garbage signatures the moment a vault became claimable, locking
  out the legitimate beneficiary at exactly the moment they need access.
  SOLUTION: the cooldown is PER-CALLER, not per-vault (one wallet's failures
  never block another wallet), while the escalating fee — paid in native
  BOT and consumed even on failure (the function returns rather than
  reverting, so failed attempts cannot refund themselves) — keeps spam
  costly.

PROBLEM 8 — CUSTODY AND LOCKUP RISK
  Solved structurally from v1 and preserved: assets never move at setup.
  The owner grants a standard allowance to the contract; the contract
  executes transferFrom only at verified claim time, sweeping directly from
  the owner's wallet to the signed destination. Fees are forwarded the
  moment they arrive. The contract's balance is zero at the end of every
  transaction; there is nothing in it to steal, freeze, or exploit. There
  is no admin key, no upgradeability, no backdoor — an upgradeable
  inheritance vault would itself be a custody backdoor.

PROBLEM 9 — REAL-WORLD MESSINESS OF TOKENS
  One malicious or broken token must not brick an entire inheritance.
  SOLUTION: skip-and-continue sweeping (each transfer in try/catch; failures
  are logged as events and skipped), a bounded per-collection NFT sweep cap
  to avoid exceeding block gas limits on huge collections, and repeatable
  claims (with fresh signatures over the incremented nonce) so leftovers
  are never stranded. Known, documented exclusions rather than silent
  failures: native BOT (no approve mechanism exists for a chain's native
  coin), ERC-1155 (roadmap), and non-enumerable ERC-721 collections.

PROBLEM 10 — HUMAN-LANGUAGE FRAGILITY
  Hashing and key derivation are byte-exact: one different space, line
  break, or invisible Unicode encoding difference produces a completely
  different key with zero partial credit — a beneficiary could hold the
  "correct" phrase and still be locked out forever. Worse, identical-looking
  text (especially kanji and accented characters) can be encoded as
  different bytes by different keyboards and operating systems.
  SOLUTION: mandatory NFC Unicode normalization at both setup and claim;
  leading/trailing whitespace trimmed while internal formatting is preserved
  as deliberate entropy; forced full re-typing confirmation of both phrases
  at setup (catching drift immediately, not years later); and a frozen,
  published cryptographic spec with shared test vectors that BOTH the
  JavaScript frontend and the Python CLI must pass in CI — eliminating the
  documented bug class where two implementations of the "same" derivation
  silently diverge.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THE PROJECT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Honesty about scope is part of the design philosophy ("honest constrained
MVP over overbuilt vaporware"):

  - It does NOT hide the vault. On a public chain, the vault's existence,
    owner address, registered tokens, and timer are visible to everyone,
    permanently. True on-chain privacy would require a fundamentally
    different (encrypted-state / ZK-native) architecture. What the design
    protects is the only thing that NEEDS protecting: the secret itself,
    which never touches the chain in any recoverable form. Pseudonymity
    (fresh single-purpose wallets) covers the practical rest.

  - It does NOT sweep native BOT. approve()/transferFrom() are ERC-20
    functions; a chain's native coin has no contract to grant allowances
    on. Documented exclusion, with wrapped-token workaround noted only if
    a canonical wrapper exists.

  - It does NOT run by itself. Smart contracts are passive; nothing fires
    automatically when a timer expires. A claimant must show up. Keeper
    automation is a documented roadmap item, not a hidden assumption.

  - It does NOT offer recovery. Losing both phrases means permanent
    lockout. No backdoor exists, because any backdoor is an attack surface.
    The setup flow generates a printable beneficiary instruction sheet
    (client-side, phrases never pre-filled) as the real-world mitigation
    for the equally real risk that a beneficiary never learns the vault
    exists.

  - It does NOT claim to be audited. Its own documentation labels it
    "reviewed design, unaudited implementation" and forbids real mainnet
    value until an independent review occurs.

5. DEPLOYMENT CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Chain:        BOT Chain — EVM-compatible L1 (Geth-API compatible,
                Parlia PoSA consensus, 0.75s blocks, fast finality)
  Active:       TESTNET, chain ID 968
                RPC https://rpc.bohr.life | Explorer https://scan.bohr.life
                Faucet https://faucet.botchain.ai/basic
  Prepared:     MAINNET, chain ID 677 — configured fail-closed (address
                null; the app refuses to guess). Because the chain ID lives
                in both the EIP-712 domain and the KDF salt, testnet vaults,
                keys, and signatures are cryptographically unusable on
                mainnet by construction; the mainnet switch is a clean
                redeploy plus one config entry.
  Stack:        Solidity ^0.8.24 + OpenZeppelin (EIP712, ECDSA,
                ReentrancyGuard); static browser frontend (ethers v6,
                WebCrypto PBKDF2, zxcvbn) with NO backend — a server that
                could ever see a phrase would itself be the vulnerability;
                narrow-scope Python CLI for independent offline
                verification of the cryptography.
  UI:           Calm, editorial, typography-led design (deep forest-black
                and warm cream, serif phrase fields treating the phrase as
                a personal artifact, soft salmon instead of harsh red for
                failure states — a failed inheritance claim is a grief
                moment, not a scolding), modeled on commonsmade.com.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DMH v2 answers the question "how do my crypto assets reach the person I
choose if I die, disappear, or lose my keys — without trusting anyone, moving
anything up front, or ever exposing the secret that guards them?" It does so
by inverting the naive design: instead of a vault that must HEAR your secret
to open (burning it into public record forever the first time it is used),
it registers two public addresses derived from two private, self-composed
phrases, and opens only for a pair of one-time cryptographic signatures that
prove knowledge of both phrases while revealing neither — bound to a specific
destination, nonce, chain, and contract so that copying, replaying, or
front-running them accomplishes nothing. The chain is the witness and the
referee; it is never the keeper of the secret, and never the keeper of the
funds.
