Scope: Contract, frontend, deployment safeguards, and documented residual risks.

IMPLEMENTED HARDENING
---------------------

1. Low-level token return data is copied only after checking its size, and no
   more than 32 bytes are copied into contract memory.
2. Immediately stale, valid previous-nonce signatures revert without charging
   a fee or applying cooldown during a five-minute race grace period.
3. Public previous-nonce signatures stop receiving the fee-free exception
   after the grace period.
4. The constructor rejects a base fee that could overflow after all configured
   fee doublings.
5. The supplied deployment script rejects a fee-recipient address that already
   contains contract code.
6. The fixed token-call gas limit is retained to constrain hostile token code.
7. Phrase mismatch feedback is combined and no longer identifies which phrase
   matched.
8. Repeated browser mismatch attempts receive a short local delay.
9. Browser private-key construction and EIP-712 signing occur inside the crypto
   worker; private keys are not returned to the main UI thread.
10. The frontend CSP excludes unsafe-inline and unsafe-eval.
11. Static-host security headers are provided in frontend/public/_headers.
12. CLAIMED vault warnings now explain that the vault remains immediately
    claimable until deactivated and approvals are revoked.

REMAINING SECURITY RISKS
------------------------

1. Weak or predictable phrases remain the largest practical compromise route.
   Public signer addresses are offline phrase-verification oracles. Browser
   throttling does not prevent independent scripted guessing.
2. A hostile or non-compliant token can report transfer success without
   changing balances or ownership. DMH sweep events are not independent proof
   of asset movement; important transfers must be verified on the token
   contract.
3. Every fee-bearing claim permanently depends on the immutable fee recipient
   accepting native BOT. The deployment check is a point-in-time safeguard,
   not a permanent on-chain guarantee.
4. Failure fees and cooldowns are caller-scoped. An attacker can use new funded
   addresses to remain at the base fee, although this does not improve offline
   phrase guessing and still costs gas and the fee.
5. The fixed 150,000 gas transfer budget may skip unusually complex legitimate
   token transfers or ERC-721 receiver hooks. Raising it would increase hostile
   token gas-griefing capacity.
6. CLAIMED vaults intentionally remain immediately claimable for follow-up
   sweeps. Newly received assets from registered contracts remain exposed under
   surviving approvals until the owner deactivates the vault and revokes them.
7. JavaScript cannot guarantee physical erasure of phrases or key material.
   Worker isolation reduces exposure but cannot protect against compromised
   same-origin code or browser execution.
8. The response-header file is effective only on hosting platforms that support
   its format. Other hosts must install equivalent CSP and security headers.
9. There is no automated browser cryptographic or wallet end-to-end suite.
10. The implementation has not received an independent professional audit,
    formal verification, or comprehensive fuzz/invariant testing.

PRE-REDEPLOYMENT FINDINGS
-------------------------

M-1. Immutable fee-recipient liveness

Every fee-bearing claim depends on the immutable fee recipient accepting native
BOT. If that transfer ever rejects, successful and failed claim attempts revert
before completion. The deployment script's no-code check is only a point-in-time
safeguard and does not prove control, prevent an address typo, or guarantee
future acceptance.

Required before redeployment:

- Use a deliberately controlled ordinary EOA.
- Independently verify the configured address in full.
- Test native BOT receipt before deployment.
- Assert the deployed immutable recipient and fee after deployment.
- Document that this dependency cannot be repaired in an immutable instance.

Testnet disposition: accepted for this deployment. The configured recipient had
no code and accepted a confirmed 1 wei native BOT transfer before deployment.
The immutable values were asserted again during the live lifecycle. The
architectural dependency remains and must be reassessed for any future address.

M-2. Worst-case complete-claim gas

Each registered token call is individually bounded, but a vault may contain 50
entries. Fifty hostile or expensive ERC-20 entries can consume approximately
11.5 million callee gas before protocol overhead. A mixed ERC-20/ERC-721 vault
can also approach practical block or RPC gas limits. Registered entries cannot
be edited after a vault becomes CLAIMED, so an expensive registry can impair
follow-up sweeps.

Required before redeployment:

- Measure a complete 50-entry hostile-token claim on BOT Chain testnet.
- Measure a worst-case mixed ERC-20/ERC-721 claim.
- Compare measured gas with BOT Chain block and RPC transaction limits.
- Reduce registry limits or introduce bounded claim pagination if the margin is
  insufficient.

Testnet disposition: accepted with measured headroom. The BOT testnet block gas
limit was 35,000,000. A 50-token near-stipend ERC-20 claim used 10,394,058 gas,
and a mixed 30 ERC-20/20 ERC-721 claim used 7,803,533 gas. Regression tests cap
both scenarios below 24,000,000 gas. Future chain or gas-schedule changes must
repeat these measurements.

M-3. Token-reported success is not settlement proof

A malicious token may return success without transferring the requested asset.
DMH sweep events report successful call semantics, not independently verified
balance or ownership changes. Operational tooling must verify settlement on the
token contract. This is documented and is not an authorization bypass.

LOW-SEVERITY AND DESIGN FINDINGS
--------------------------------

1. Signer addresses are not required on-chain to be EOAs. A misconfigured
   contract signer may make a vault permanently unclaimable until the owner
   deactivates it and creates another vault.
2. Caller-scoped failure penalties can be bypassed with fresh funded accounts.
3. CLAIMED vaults remain immediately claimable under surviving approvals.
4. Native BOT forced into the contract cannot be recovered.
5. Complex legitimate token calls may exceed the fixed 150,000 gas stipend and
   be skipped.
6. Token registrations and approvals remain exposed to upgradeable or hostile
   token implementations.

HARDENING VALIDATED
-------------------

1. No replay can move assets with an old nonce.
2. The five-minute stale-signature path only refunds a legitimate immediate
   race and cannot execute another sweep.
3. After five minutes, stale public calldata follows the normal fee/cooldown
   path.
4. Return-data copy operations are bounded to 32 bytes and do not allocate a
   dynamic return buffer.
5. Fee doubling is capped and constructor-bounded against arithmetic overflow.
6. Reentrancy through ERC-721 receiver hooks remains blocked.
7. Deactivation remains irreversible and owner-only.
8. The signed destination prevents a relayer from redirecting a copied claim.

MISSING PRE-PRODUCTION TESTS
----------------------------

1. Reverting or gas-consuming fee recipient.
2. Maximum 50-token hostile registry gas measurement.
3. Maximum mixed ERC-20/ERC-721 registry gas measurement.
4. ERC-721 receiver near and beyond the transfer gas limit.
5. Tokens that report success without changing balances or ownership.
6. Exact stale-grace boundary timestamp.
7. Multiple successful follow-up claims and signatures older than one nonce.
8. Forced native BOT behavior.
9. State-machine, nonce-monotonicity, registry, and liveVaultOf invariant tests.
10. Post-deployment BOT testnet EIP-712 claim smoke test.

is not approved by this review yet. Before handling real value, add the remaining
adversarial and invariant tests and obtain an independent Solidity audit.
